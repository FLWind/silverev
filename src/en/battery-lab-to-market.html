Assessing battery developments

From a lab cell to an electric vehicle: what still needs to work

A record material, a successful battery and a mass-market car are different achievements. What connects them, and why does it matter for silver demand?

27 September 20267 min read

First question: what was actually tested?

‘Silver improved the battery’ can describe a tougher electrolyte, more uniform lithium deposition or the operation of a complete cell. These results answer different questions. To assess industrial demand, we need to understand which steps remain.

From a material property to a finished product
Test objectWhat it can establishWhat still needs testing
Material or coatingStrength, conductivity and surface behaviour.Performance inside a rechargeable cell.
Symmetric cell or half-cellThe behaviour of a selected electrode or interface.Lithium and energy balance in a full cell.
Full laboratory cellPositive and negative electrodes operating together.Practical size, design and reproducibility.
Multilayer automotive cellTechnology operating in a format closer to a product.Manufacturing yield, integration and vehicle requirements.
Pack and evaluation vehicleSystem operation with mounting, cooling and controls.Consistent mass production, cost and long-term use.

A useful example is the Stanford research announced by the university in January 2026. Silver treatment of a ceramic electrolyte surface improved small samples' resistance to cracking. The authors explicitly identified testing in full cells as a future step. This is a valuable material result, but not yet a measurement of automotive battery life. [1]

Performance figures need their test conditions

To compare developments, compile a short experimental profile: cell format and capacity, active material per unit area, electrolyte thickness, lithium inventory, temperature, current, operating pressure and the number of samples tested. Missing information should narrow the conclusion.

A thin cathode may make a new interface easier to test while providing little energy for the same coated area. Excess lithium can compensate for losses and obscure the difficulties of an anode-free design. A strong result at elevated temperature still needs comparison with vehicle operating conditions. These parameters help identify the problem the researchers actually solved.

Cycle counts also need context: depth of discharge, charging rate, remaining capacity and temperature. A thousand shallow cycles are not a thousand full cycles, and a short intensive test does not replace years of calendar ageing. It is particularly useful to see fast charging and long cycle life demonstrated on the same version of a cell.

Pressure becomes part of the vehicle

Solid-state batteries must maintain contact between solid components. Operating pressure is therefore a property of the whole design, not a minor detail in a paper. It should be distinguished from pressure applied once during manufacturing.

An illustrative calculation shows the scale. A pressure of 2 MPa over 100 cm² produces a force of F = P × A = 20,000 N, approximately two tonnes-force. This is neither the battery's mass nor a requirement for any specific technology. It illustrates the load that the retaining structure must transmit.

When assessing a product, ask how contact is maintained, what happens as electrode volume changes and how much space the mechanical system occupies. A material's volumetric energy advantage may shrink once these components are included. Pack-level comparisons therefore address automotive requirements more directly than comparisons of individual active layers.

One successful cell does not establish manufacturing yield

In a 2024 study, 21 research groups received the same starting materials. They followed a specified electrochemical test protocol but used their own assembly methods. Results varied considerably, leading the authors to recommend fuller reporting of parameters and replicate measurements. [2]

This suggests a simple question when reading an announcement: are we seeing the best cell or a distribution of results? Sample count, failure rate and performance variation matter. Manufacturing adds coating uniformity over large areas, batch consistency and defect control.

A pilot line helps investigate these issues. Its existence does not automatically disclose cost or throughput. The next meaningful achievement is consistent output of qualified cells with performance accepted by a customer.

Scale-up can affect silver demand in both directions

Ag may provide a valuable interfacial improvement, but a mass-market product must justify both the material and the process used to apply it. Manufacturers have an incentive to find the lowest loading that preserves the benefit. Better technology can therefore expand adoption while reducing grams of silver per kWh.

A material balance should distinguish silver in finished cells, metal in process scrap and material returned to production. Layer area, composition and manufacturing yield matter more than the impression created by the word ‘silver’. A thin film over a large total area can be significant, but its mass cannot be established without numbers.

Samsung's industrial programme illustrates another necessary step: involving an automotive customer. The October 2025 agreement between BMW, Samsung SDI and Solid Power covers building and evaluating cells for eventual integration into test vehicles. This is a specific validation stage, with further work remaining before mass deliveries. [3]

That is why our EV demand model needs two separate inputs: output of batteries using the technology and Ag loading per unit of energy. Growth in the first does not guarantee proportional growth in metal consumption if the second falls.

Five questions for the next announcement

  1. What was tested? A material, an interface, a full cell, a pack or a vehicle?
  2. Are the conditions disclosed? Capacity, temperature, pressure, charging rate and the lifetime criterion.
  3. Is the result reproducible? How many samples were tested, and how much did results vary?
  4. Is silver's role known? What does it improve, and is there a measured Ag loading?
  5. What supports the scale-up claim? Customer samples, an operating line, qualified output or commercial deliveries?

The answers reveal what a publication changes. A material result strengthens confidence in the mechanism. Product testing brings an application closer. Consistent output and disclosed metal loading make it possible to update a quantitative demand estimate.
