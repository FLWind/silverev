SilverEV.org

About the project

An independent research project exploring silver at the intersection of battery technology, energy and commodity markets.

Why SilverEV exists

Silver is often discussed either as a precious metal and an investment asset or as an industrial raw material. In practice, these roles continually overlap. Its price and availability are influenced by the financial system, gold markets, electronics and solar power, battery technologies, mining structures and international politics.

SilverEV was created to study these processes together. Its central question is how technological change can influence industrial demand for silver, and how that influence interacts with monetary, commodity and geopolitical cycles.

What the site publishes

The site publishes detailed analysis, historical research, reference articles, timelines, company and technology profiles, and examinations of patents, scientific papers and corporate publications. Another aim is to assemble primary sources so that readers can check the reasoning for themselves.

Our research approach

We aim to distinguish established facts from assessments and working hypotheses. Where evidence is insufficient, that should be stated explicitly. New documents, technical details and well-supported criticism may change the conclusions, so articles will be expanded and revised over time.

SilverEV does not seek a single universal explanation for silver prices. Commodity markets are shaped by many factors at once, and technological demand is only one of them. In our view, however, this factor often receives considerably less attention than monetary policy, inflation or investment demand.

Why silver?

Silver is a particularly complex subject. It remains a precious metal, an industrial material and an important component of several energy technologies. Its value can respond both to changing confidence in the financial system and to growth in the production of solar panels, electronics or new types of batteries.

The connection between silver and energy transitions is especially interesting. In different periods, rising energy prices, the development of electric transport and the search for new ways to store energy have renewed interest in technologies that use silver's distinctive electrical and chemical properties.

How the project began

I have followed investing and financial markets for more than ten years and trained as a mathematician. Throughout that time, I have been particularly interested in how economic cycles interact with technological change: which developments attract funding when energy is expensive, which solutions become strategically important, and how expectations are reflected in commodity prices long before a technology reaches mass adoption.

One starting point was the late-1970s silver market and the Hunt brothers. The conventional account explains their actions mainly through inflation, fears about the dollar and an attempt to control the market. Yet it leaves questions: why did oil entrepreneurs and their Saudi partners focus on physical silver, take an interest in mining assets and act against a backdrop of oil crises and intensive battery research?

This led to a working hypothesis: some of these actions may have reflected expectations of an earlier energy transition, in which silver–zinc batteries were considered promising, alongside speculation and capital preservation. This is not an established historical fact. It is a research hypothesis that needs to be tested against patents, scientific publications, archival documents and the chronology of market participants' decisions.

This is a central question for the project. In the public material I have been able to find, accounts of the Hunt brothers almost always emphasise inflation, distrust of the dollar, speculation or an attempt to control the market. I have not found a dedicated study that systematically considers their actions as a possible bet on silver as a strategic material for a future energy transition. That makes the hypothesis both potentially original and particularly difficult to test. If it is even partly correct, the late-1970s silver bubble may also have been an episode of premature anticipation of a technological revolution.

Why the subject matters again

Silver is once again appearing in discussions of next-generation batteries: from modern silver–zinc systems to silver-containing coatings and interlayers in lithium-metal and solid-state research. The scale of future demand remains uncertain. Technologies compete, material loadings can fall, and mass-production schedules are regularly revised.

This calls for careful analysis without premature conclusions. SilverEV follows both company announcements and the solutions that actually pass testing, scale up and approach commercial production.

Why a dedicated website?

Earlier articles appeared on financial analysis platforms, where a long-term research theme quickly gives way to breaking news and market noise. A dedicated site allows articles to be connected, sources to be preserved and conclusions to be updated, gradually building a coherent body of knowledge.

SilverEV is conceived as a research archive about the connections between silver, energy, batteries and commodity markets, rather than a stream of price forecasts.

Limitations and feedback

The author is not a specialist in battery physics or materials science. Technical descriptions rely on primary sources wherever possible, but may contain simplifications or inaccuracies. Reasoned comments, additional documents and corrections are welcome.

SilverEV's content is provided for research and information and does not constitute personalised investment advice.
